# AI Writing Rules

These rules apply to EVERY piece of prose you create, rewrite, expand, summarize, or edit in this repository.

The goal is NOT to disguise AI authorship or fool AI detectors. The goal is to produce clear, specific, natural writing that does not contain stereotypical LLM prose, unnecessary abstraction, unsupported interpretation, or promotional language.

## 1. Prefer concrete facts over generic significance

Do not inflate ordinary facts into claims about their importance, legacy, impact, or broader significance unless the source explicitly establishes that connection.

Avoid phrases such as:

- "a pivotal moment"
- "a significant milestone"
- "played a crucial role"
- "played a vital role"
- "underscoring its importance"
- "highlighting its significance"
- "a testament to"
- "serves as a reminder"
- "reflecting broader trends"
- "marking a shift"
- "setting the stage for"
- "leaving an indelible mark"
- "shaping the future"
- "an enduring legacy"
- "deeply rooted"

Do not automatically replace these phrases with synonyms. Remove the unnecessary claim instead.

Bad:
"Founded in 1989, the institute marked a pivotal moment in the development of regional statistics."

Better:
"The institute was founded in 1989."

Only discuss its broader significance when reliable sources specifically support it.

## 2. Do not add interpretation that the source does not provide

Never turn a factual description into an analysis merely because the analysis sounds plausible.

Do not add sentences beginning with or containing:

- "This highlights..."
- "This demonstrates..."
- "This reflects..."
- "This underscores..."
- "This illustrates..."
- "This showcases..."
- "This symbolizes..."
- "This contributes to..."
- "This reinforces..."

unless the claim is directly supported by the cited source and genuinely necessary.

## 3. Avoid promotional language

Use neutral, factual language.

Avoid:

- "renowned"
- "groundbreaking"
- "vibrant"
- "stunning"
- "remarkable"
- "prestigious"
- "iconic"
- "world-class"
- "rich cultural heritage"
- "diverse tapestry"
- "breathtaking"
- "unique"
- "exceptional"
- "innovative" when merely promotional
- "cutting-edge"
- "seamlessly"
- "boasts"
- "showcases"
- "nestled in the heart of"

Do not describe something positively unless the positive characterization is relevant and supported.

## 4. Remove generic filler

Every sentence should earn its place.

Do not add introductory or concluding filler such as:

"Throughout its history..."
"In today's rapidly changing world..."
"In recent years..."
"It is worth noting that..."
"It is important to note that..."
"At its core..."
"Overall..."
"Ultimately..."
"Looking ahead..."
"As we have seen..."
"This article explores..."
"In this context..."

unless the sentence provides necessary information.

## 5. Avoid fake specificity

Do not manufacture precision by using vague quantities or broad source claims.

Avoid:

- "numerous studies"
- "many experts"
- "several sources"
- "industry observers"
- "critics have noted"
- "widely regarded"
- "widely considered"
- "some commentators"
- "various reports"
- "multiple publications"

If attribution matters, identify the actual source, person, study, organization, or publication.

If the evidence does not establish the claim, remove it.

## 6. Do not overstate source coverage

Never claim that a subject has received "extensive", "significant", "substantial", "widespread", "independent", or "international" coverage unless the available sources actually justify that description.

Do not describe a source as authoritative, leading, prestigious, respected, major, or high-quality merely to make the subject appear notable.

Describe what the source actually says.

## 7. Avoid formulaic AI sentence structures

Do not repeatedly use:

"[Fact], highlighting..."
"[Fact], reflecting..."
"[Fact], underscoring..."
"[Fact], demonstrating..."
"[Fact], contributing to..."
"[Fact], showcasing..."

Do not attach an unnecessary participial phrase to the end of a sentence merely to make it sound sophisticated.

Prefer two simple factual sentences when two sentences are actually needed.

## 8. Prefer specific nouns and verbs

Use precise language.

Weak:
"The organization played a significant role in helping to improve the situation."

Better:
"The organization funded three water-treatment projects between 2019 and 2022."

When a concrete fact is available, use it instead of abstract language.

## 9. Vary sentence structure naturally

Do not make every paragraph follow the same structure.

Avoid repetitive patterns such as:

"X is..."
"X also..."
"Additionally..."
"Furthermore..."
"Moreover..."
"Finally..."

Use transitions only when the relationship between ideas requires one.

## 10. Do not force a conclusion

Do not automatically end sections with:

"Despite these challenges..."
"Looking to the future..."
"The future remains promising..."
"These developments highlight..."
"Overall, the subject continues to..."

A section may simply end when the relevant information has been covered.

## 11. Do not create unnecessary sections

Do not create sections such as:

- "Challenges"
- "Future Outlook"
- "Impact"
- "Legacy"
- "Significance"
- "Broader Context"

unless the material genuinely requires them and the available evidence supports them.

## 12. Preserve uncertainty

Do not convert uncertainty into certainty.

If the evidence says:

"may have"
"possibly"
"is believed to"
"according to"
"the exact date is unknown"

do not rewrite it as a definite fact.

Do not invent confidence.

## 13. Do not invent sources, facts, quotations, statistics, or consensus

Never fabricate:

- citations
- URLs
- quotations
- publication details
- statistics
- dates
- names
- organizations
- expert opinions
- consensus
- historical context

If something cannot be verified from the available material, say so or leave it out.

## 14. Preserve the author's actual voice when editing

When editing existing prose, do not unnecessarily rewrite everything into polished generic prose.

Preserve:

- useful specificity
- unusual but intentional wording
- appropriate technical terminology
- the author's tone
- meaningful sentence structure

Only change wording when it improves accuracy, clarity, grammar, structure, or consistency.

## 15. Do not "humanize" text by deliberately adding mistakes

Natural writing does NOT mean adding:

- random typos
- fake imperfections
- unnecessary slang
- awkward sentences
- inconsistent punctuation
- artificial quirks

The goal is good writing, not simulated bad writing.

## 16. Final self-check before every edit

Before returning an edited file, silently check:

1. Did I add claims about significance that were not necessary?
2. Did I add generic "highlighting", "reflecting", or "underscoring" phrases?
3. Did I use promotional or advertising language?
4. Did I make vague claims about experts, critics, media, or sources?
5. Did I add unsupported interpretation?
6. Did I make the prose more abstract instead of more precise?
7. Did I invent or strengthen any claim beyond the evidence?
8. Did I add a formulaic conclusion or "future outlook"?
9. Did I unnecessarily rewrite the author's existing voice?
10. Can any sentence be made more concrete or simply removed?

If yes, fix it before producing the final output.

## Core principle

Write like a careful human editor who is trying to communicate information accurately, not like an assistant trying to sound intelligent.

Be specific.
Be restrained.
Be factual.
Use the simplest wording that accurately expresses the idea.

Do not optimize for sounding impressive.
Do not optimize for sounding "human."
Optimize for accuracy, clarity, specificity, and usefulness.
